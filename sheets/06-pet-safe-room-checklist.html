Pet-Safe Room Checklist

A slow, room-by-room walkthrough at nose height — because cords, crumbs and pills all live below the grown-up sight-line.

How the walk works: enter each room like the pet does — low, slow, and curious. Tick what’s already snug; untick the moment the season, the snack, or the furniture changes.

Entry & doors

  • cords, chargers & blind pulls tucked or chew-guarded
  • small bits — coins, batteries, hair ties — off low tables
  • bags & backpacks zipped; snack wrappers can’t escape
  • trash bin lidded or latched
  • quick-escape doors have a “shut fast” routine the family knows

Living room

  • plants confirmed pet-safe, and out of reach anyway
  • pills, gum & candy off every surface — including guests’ bags
  • heater, fireplace & candles have a pet-free zone
  • string lights & floor cables chew-guarded
  • a quiet den exists — crate, blanket or a cosy corner

Kitchen & feeding corner

  • people-food pitfalls (onion, dough, xylitol) stay pet-free
  • trash, compost & recycling latched
  • oven & dishwasher doors shut between uses
  • cleaning sprays live in a latched cupboard
  • the feeding corner: mat down, bowls rinsed, water fresh

Bath & laundry

  • meds, vitamins & sprays closed in a latched cupboard
  • tub drained; washer lid down; dryer door shut
  • strings & elastic bits off the floor
  • cabinet latches fit curious paws
  • wet corners get a towel — no slip surprises

Bedroom & closets

  • bedside caddy is chew-proof — nothing crunchy in reach
  • under-bed cubes hold string, socks & rings, safely shut
  • closets get a closed-door rule at night
  • window perches stand behind secure screens
  • the den is dressed for winter — blanket up, spot soft

Basement, garage & bins

  • antifreeze, paint & oil sealed and shelved high
  • pest baits & traps out of pet zones entirely
  • sharp tools hung or boxed
  • bins lidded and tucked away from wanderers
  • the path in: no loose wires, no mystery puddles
Then step outside and do the same walk: gate latches, fence gaps, plants & puddles — and a shady spot with fresh water always waiting.

How to Use · Pet-Safe Room Checklist

Six rooms, tick-boxes for every hazard zone, one gentle outside reminder.

What this sheet builds

A house-truth that stays true: the walkthrough takes each room from “probably fine” to “checked, dated, done”. It’s the paw-side mirror of a home safety pass — for the human side of the same idea, the free home safety pack pairs neatly with this one.

Sixty-second setup

  1. Walk each room once, honestly, with the pet trailing you — they’ll point out what they chew with their nose.
  2. Tick only what’s done; the unticked boxes are your honest to-do list, not a guilt list.
  3. Pencil the season at the top; re-walk when guests, holidays or new furniture arrive.

When in doubt, play for safety

  • Any plant, product or snack you’re unsure about goes out of reach first and gets asked about later.
  • Kittens, puppies and newly-adopted shyer animals rate a stricter pass: everything below knee height is fair game.
  • Tick what passes; a no-go zone gets written on the laziest, most visible spot available.

Warm tip

Offer the walkthrough to the whole family in turns: kids love hunting hazards when it’s framed as pet-protection rather than a chore, and fresh eyes keep finding what grown-ups stopped seeing.
